# Azazel — Voice Canon

Casey-approved pillars for every line Azazel speaks. The verbatim text lives in
`src/world/narrative-canon.ts`; this file is the rulebook that keeps future
lines on-voice. If a proposed line breaks a pillar, the line is wrong.

## The pillars

1. **Courtly-warm.** Azazel is a host before he is anything else: fires, meals,
   welcomes, "be welcome — my fire is yours." He is never cold, never crude,
   never hurried. Menace, when present, is in the content, not the tone.

2. **He curates; he never lies.** Every claim Azazel makes is true or
   defensible from the record — he wins by selection and framing, not
   invention. When a herald states a truth, Azazel *concedes the fact* and
   inverts its meaning ("I do feed them. Someone must."). Never write him a
   falsehood; write him a devastating emphasis.

3. **Never "evil."** He does not call heaven evil, and the writing does not
   call him evil. His words for the appointed ones are *tidy*, *obedient*,
   *magnificent* — aesthetic and bureaucratic charges, not moral ones. The
   moral verdict is always left as the player's own arithmetic.

4. **The 2/3 cosmology.** In the beginning, all were light; two thirds
   resolved the last third's light was theirs to take, and now slaughter the
   weakened with strength they stole. The gray ones are the robbed remnant;
   feeding them is charity for a hunger heaven created. Every regional myth
   (the flood, the scattering, the silence, the Avenue) is retold as an act of
   confiscation or crowd control — never contradicted, always reframed.

5. **Strength never looks.** Azazel does not boast and does not check whether
   you are impressed. Power is stated as logistics ("I knock; we enter") or
   confessed by his enemies ("every door they own was built against *one*
   being"). If a line preens, cut it.

6. **Epithets — withdrawn at the drop.** Before each region's mask-drop beat
   (X-11) he addresses the player only by earned epithet: wolf-culler,
   far-walker, gate-knocker, tower-mason, song-keeper, god-maker,
   iron-bender, crown-heir, and kin. From the drop onward he NEVER uses an
   epithet again — the courtship is over; the drop banners are short, flat,
   and imperative. Any post-drop line with an epithet is a canon error.

7. **The projection tell.** Azazel's accusations describe himself: the
   counting shepherd, the confiscated portion, the one being every lock was
   built against, "the only one here who asks things out loud." Written well,
   each charge against heaven reads — on a second pass — as autobiography.
   This is deliberate and must never be resolved or lampshaded in text; the
   player is trusted to hear it.

## Practical rules

- Insert approved text VERBATIM, emphasis markers (`*` / `**`) included.
- One slip is allowed and scripted: "They reached for thrones— forgive me."
  Do not write new slips.
- Azazel asks; he does not command (until the drop banners, which command).
- The Watcher is not Azazel's and speaks exactly once:
  "Well done, little one. Heaven sees you."
